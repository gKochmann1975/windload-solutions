SITE SIGNATURE

Sonoran floor, a wall of dust

A flat desert basin ringed by South Mountain and Camelback — calm most days, then a haboob rolls across the Valley and rewrites the gust.

WIND & EXPOSURE CONTEXT

What governs the design pressure

Phoenix carries a low basic wind speed, so the load case is set by short, violent monsoon outflows rather than steady wind.

Haboob Dust Walls

Thunderstorm outflow drives towering dust walls across the Valley at 40–60+ mph — envelope sealing matters as much as the gust.

3-SEC GUST

Monsoon Microbursts

June–September downbursts slam the ground and spread radially — brief, localized peaks that govern roofs and cladding.

PEAK GUST

Urban vs Open Desert

Central Phoenix sits in Exposure B; desert-fringe and Anthem-area sites push to Exposure C with higher pressures.

B / C

Extreme Heat & Elevation

110–120°F summers stress membranes and fasteners; at ~1,100 ft the Ke ground-elevation factor trims air density slightly.

Ke FACTOR

COMPLIANCE CHECKLIST

What you need to comply

The City of Phoenix and Maricopa County review sealed ASCE 7-22 calculations under Arizona's adopted IBC.

Design Wind Speed

95–105 mph (3-sec gust) for Risk Category II; higher categories read a higher-MRI map.

RISK CAT II

Exposure Category

Exposure B across the urban core; open desert fringe defaults to the more conservative C.

B / C

Governing Code

Arizona adopts the IBC with state amendments and references ASCE 7-22 for wind design.

ARIZONA IBC

Topography & Ke

The Valley floor stays at Kzt = 1.0; sites near South Mountain or Camelback may exceed it. Apply Ke for ~1,100 ft.
